Quick Heating and Cooling Help in Seattle

When your system quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep our schedule flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls typically see a technician within a day, and we treat no-heat and no-cool calls as top priority.

The emergency calls we answer tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, clogged condensate drains backing water into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Because our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls get fixed in one visit, at a price you approve before we start.

Two situations call for a quick step from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or produces a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and give us a call. That small step can save you from a much larger repair.

Keeping Your Seattle HVAC System Running Longer

Most mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repairs stacking up season after season, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one during the first heat wave of summer.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.
