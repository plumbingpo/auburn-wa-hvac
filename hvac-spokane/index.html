Keeping Your Spokane HVAC System Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a system that runs for years and one that breaks down in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your system? Its age says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and is also showing higher bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

The earlier you report a comfort problem, the cheaper it usually is. A weak capacitor costs little to replace, yet that same capacitor, ignored through a full season, can ruin the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away as soon as something seems off in your home. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Spokane

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of problems. Long cooling seasons push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and salt air and moisture chew at outdoor condenser coils and fins, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Dirty filters quietly choke off airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

Our technicians also run into the full range of component failures in local homes: weak capacitors that leave the condenser humming without starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, refrigerant leaks that turn into long lukewarm afternoons, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's mix of older homes brings undersized returns, leaky duct runs, and rooms the original system was never designed to condition.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much less expensive when caught early.
